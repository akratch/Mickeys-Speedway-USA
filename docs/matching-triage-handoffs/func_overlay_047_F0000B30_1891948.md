#### Localization packet, 2026-09-09

This packet did not match the function. It converted four assumptions into
measurements, closed three of them as identities, eliminated two hypotheses,
and moved the masked residual from 1,972 to 1,906 differing words. It also
reclassifies the residual: the workbench's `structure-mismatch` verdict is an
artifact of positional comparison, and the disagreement is register
allocation, not structure.

**Classification, measured three ways.** Aligning the two instruction streams
under progressively weaker keys separates selection from allocation:

- keyed on the mnemonic alone: 2025 of 2168 aligned, 93.4%.
- keyed on mnemonic plus register class, blind to stack displacements:
  1771 of 2168, 81.7%.
- keyed on mnemonic plus register identity: 648 of 2168, 29.9%.

Instruction selection is therefore essentially correct; almost the whole gap
opens when register identity is added. The cumulative drift between the two
streams stays inside +-16 words across the entire 8,672-byte body and returns
to zero repeatedly, so there is no long-range structural displacement: the
residual re-converges everywhere. Positional word equality is a poor score for
this function precisely because a pervasive renaming destroys it.

**Compiler flags: settled, and two hypotheses eliminated.** The whole lattice
was compiled and scored (119 combinations, 53 scored). `-O2 -mips2 -32` with
no extras -- the flags `mk/overlays.mk` already gives this object -- is the
unique optimum, and the only group that is both size-exact and best-scoring.
`-Wab,-r4300_mul` costs four bytes of size and thirteen words even though
`mul_scheduler_scan.py` names this TU (one pair, one nop, its weakest possible
signal): eliminated. `-Wo,-loopunroll,0` costs 968 bytes and `-Wo,-loopunroll,2`
240, so the target's loops are unrolled at the default and the sibling
`func_overlay_047_F0000000_1890E18`'s unroll override does not extend here:
eliminated. `-Wo,-loopunroll,4` is byte-identical to no extra, confirming 4 is
the default. `-O2 -g3` and both other ISAs are worse. No further flag work is
justified on this TU.

**Frame partition: now an identity, and it was not one before.** IDO assigns a
home to every declared local in declaration order, descending from the frame
top, and the law is exact here: the previous candidate's source order
predicted sixteen observed home offsets with no residual, including all three
matrix bases, the height pointer, the pair passed to the overlay 45 pair
reader, and every spilled scalar. Reading the target's homes back through the
same law fixes the original order: 34 declared words precede the first `MtxF`,
the last two of them the float pair carrying the rate spill and the saved FOV;
the three matrices follow; two words, then the height pointer, then five more
close a list of ninety words. Reordering to that census put every observable
home on the target's -- matrix bases at frame+440/+376/+312, height at +300,
the pair at +560/+556, rate at +508, FOV at +504 -- with access counts
agreeing one for one and all four prologue zero stores landing on the target's
slots. The previously reported "frame exact" was only the 640-byte total: the
partition inside it was wrong on two counts that happened to cancel.

**Player record: 0x34, and the scroll member is phantom.** The reconstruction
gave the player record a texture-scroll pointer that the function never reads;

**The residual is one FP colouring fact.** Callee-saved float use agrees
exactly at f20 (27 uses), f22 (10), f24 (7) and f28 (13). The target has a
sixth callee-saved double and we have five: its f30 carries the same web our
f26 carries, so its f26 is a web we never create. That web is a pool constant
hoisted into the entry block. The consequences cascade in a way that explains
most of what is left:

- the sixth save adds eight bytes to the save area, which is why the target's
  integer save block sits eight bytes above ours even though the frame totals
  agree;
- the hoist changes which pool constant is referenced first, and the two
  pools' *internal* geometry is identical -- two floats, the 24-byte dispatch
  table, four floats, at relative offsets 0/4/32/36/40/44 on both sides -- so
  the earlier "first two pool values reversed" reading is confirmed and is a
  *consequence* of the hoist, not an independent defect;
- we spill three scalars the target keeps in registers, and emit nine more
  word stores overall.

The constant in question is the one assigned to the movement interpolation
factor in the not-leaving/not-active branch.

Two of IDO's three placement rules were isolated by measurement, and both
behave the same in our build as in the target:

- **Multi-reference constants are lifted to their common dominator.** A
  controlled probe swapped the reference counts of the animation-frame
  threshold pair. The constant that gained three references took pool slot 0
  and the entry-block register; the one that dropped to a single reference
  fell to slot 32 and was loaded at its use site. The pool is therefore
  ordered by first *emitted* reference, which is why hoisted constants come
  first and why our first two entries are in the opposite order to the
  target's. That ordering is a symptom, not a defect to fix directly.
- **Single-reference loop-invariant loads are hoisted when unconditional.**
  The icon-scale divisor is referenced once, and both sides hoist it to the
  icon loop's preheader. Our build puts it in the register the rate spill
  vacates; the target puts it in the second live range of the register whose
  first range holds the movement constant.

What does not follow either rule is the movement constant: one reference,
inside a conditional, hoisted by the target and not by us. Two spellings that
would create a second reference were tested and both are **eliminated**:
moving the assignment above the branch so that it dominates collapses the
branch and costs 84 words; adding a dominating assignment before the loop is
dead and is removed before common-subexpression elimination can see it, with
no effect on any measurement.

That leaves two causal orders, which this packet could not separate. Either
the original references this literal a second time in a live position not yet
found, and the resulting web takes the sixth callee-saved double; or the zero
web takes the sixth register for an independent numbering reason, and the
freed register is what lets the compiler speculate the conditional hoist. The
instrumented uopt profile named in the colouring-lever handoff
(`CDX_LOG=1`, `CDX_DETAIL_WEB`) is the instrument that can distinguish them,
by showing whether the hoist was considered and declined; it should be the
next packet's first move, before any further source spelling.
