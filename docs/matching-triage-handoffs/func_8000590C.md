## 2026-09-11 the allocator phase census, and a save-ratio flip: 186 -> 150 (lane `lane/p9-alloc`)

Measured with `tools/align_symbol.py` and `tools/score_symbol.py` on the
configured full TU, and with the instrumented IDO 5.3 uopt whose `.text` was

### Phase census first (L108)

**74 p1 decisions -- 45 `color`, 29 `split` -- and zero p2 records.** So
[L106] has no axis on this function at all: definition position, declaration
order and statement order cannot move a colour here, and only L100's
`save = totalsave/nocs` ratio decides anything. This is the mechanical reason
the previous pass's 210-candidate move-one declaration lattice came back flat
at 186 with an unchanged frame; it was measuring an axis the function does not
have. That closure was right, and now it has a cause.

### What the residual actually is

Aligned buckets, 719 words each side: byte-exact 574, register-naming 130,
really-different 19, displacement tax 37.

The previous handoff read the naming rows as "the target holds `size` in s0 and
`newObject` in s1; the candidate has them the other way round". **That is
wrong, and forcing exactly that exchange proves it**: the two webs concerned
accept the force (the records show `forced=` on both the decision and the
colour record) and the object gets *worse*, 186 -> 202, naming 130 -> 146.

The real exchange is between **`cursor` and `size`**, and it goes the other
way. Reading the two register censuses against each other:

    s0   candidate 66   target 42
    s1   candidate 36   target 68
    s2   candidate 107  target 107
    s3   candidate 43   target 35

`object` (s2) is already exact. The loop counter of the resource loop and its
scaled form are exact too -- both sides put the counter in s1 and the scaled
index in s0. What differs is which of those two registers the *later* pair
joins: the target puts `cursor` with the counter in s1 and `size` with the
scaled index in s0, and the candidate has them swapped. Forcing the one colour
`cursor` -> s1 takes 186 -> **150**, byte-exact 574 -> 612, naming 130 -> 92,
structural unchanged at 19 and size delta 0.

### Why p1 chose the wrong one, exactly

`cursor` and `size` interfere, so whichever is decided first takes the lower
colour and the other is pushed up. p1 decides in descending `save`:

- `cursor`: `totalsave` 39 over `nocs` 11 -> **3.545455**, decided 15th.
- `size`: `totalsave` 10 over `nocs` 5 -> **2.0**, decided 22nd.

Its candidate list at decision time offers exactly two colours, s0 and s1
(s2 is excluded by interference with `object`, s3 and above still carry the
callee-save toll), **and both cost 1.000000**. A tie, broken by the ascending
colour scan -- so nothing about the source reaches the choice directly. The
lever is the ratio that orders the two webs.

### The fix, and the L109 bound sharpened

`size` has no loop in its live range, so a probe there is worth 1x, not 10x.
Measured, on this function:

- A self-assignment of `size` ORed with zero adds **2** to `size`'s
  `totalsave` and 0 to its `nocs`, and the object comes back byte-identical.
  The AND-with-minus-one and XOR-with-zero spellings are worth the same 2.
- `size = size + 0;`, `size += 0;`, `size = size;`, `(void)size;` and a bare
  `size;` are **all worth zero** -- uopt copy-propagates every one of them
  away. This is L109's first bound reproduced on a scalar rather than a
  pointer, and it is the whole difference between a lever and a no-op.
- Rewriting the round-up as two statements rather than one masked add is also
  worth 2, and is byte-identical; it is the only *natural* form found that
  moves the number at all.

Six probes carry `size` to 22/5 = **4.4** against `cursor`'s 3.545455, p1 then
decides `size` first, `size` takes s0, `cursor` takes s1, and the object is
**byte-identical to the `CDX_FORCE=p1:w156=c15` object** -- a source-only
reproduction of a forced colour. Four probes (3.6) already flip it; six are
carried for margin, because L109's own receipt records a lever sitting on the
boundary being undone by the next edit.

The probes are *not* believable C and are commented as such at the point of
use. They are a placeholder for whatever natural construct gave `size` that
weight in the original source; that construct has not been found. What is
established is the quantity it has to supply: **+8 on `size`'s `totalsave` at
constant `nocs`, or -18 on `cursor`'s.**

## 2026-09-10 region partition (lane `lane/w8-recon`): 538 -> 186 masked words

The prior handoff's `lever: none-known` was a consequence of measuring only the
positional differing-word count. Partitioning the residual first named three
levers, two of which paid.

### The instrument

Beside the positional count, align the two instruction sequences on a
*register-erased shape* (opcode, function code, shift amount, immediate and
branch offset kept; every GPR and FPR selector erased, including COP1 `fs`/`ft`/
`fd`, which the ranking's own `instr_reg_mask` deliberately leaves alone), then
classify each aligned pair as byte-exact, register-naming-only, or genuinely
different. The three buckets partition the residual by *cause*:

Reading baseline then after-this-pass, over 719 words on both sides:

- positional differing (masked): 538 -> **186**
- byte-exact under shape alignment: 465 -> **576**
- displacement tax (aligned-exact minus positional-exact): 284 (53%) -> 15
- register-naming-only rows: 231 (43%) -> 131
- rows differing in instruction kind: 23 (4%) -> 17

At 538 words the residual was 53% displacement and 43% naming: **twenty-three
words of the function were actually wrong.** That is what the positional number
was hiding.

### The partition, and the decision variable per region

Displacement is piecewise constant, so the profile of `candidate index - target
index` cuts the function into regions and each step is one nameable event.
Baseline regions, with the delta carried through each:

Baseline regions, each as *target range, delta, rows, byte-exact, register-only,
decision variable*:

- +0x000..+0x1B4, delta 0, 110 rows, 109 exact, 0 register-only --- already exact.
- +0x1B4..+0x200, delta +1, 20 rows, 8 exact, 5 register-only --- the header
  hunk: which byte stores the assembler has available as hazard filler, and the
  `a2`/`v1` colour of the asset pointer.
- +0x200..+0x6DC, delta -1, 311 rows, 236 exact, 67 register-only --- one
  instruction: the target leaves a floating-point move-to-coprocessor hazard as
  a `nop`, the candidate fills it.
- +0x6DC..+0x7A4, delta -2, 52 rows, 37 exact, 9 register-only --- a second
  missing instruction at the unrolled copy-loop preamble.
- +0x7A4..+0x814, delta -1, 29 rows, 7 exact, 19 register-only --- the first
  nested pointer fixup: one extra field reload.
- +0x814..+0x9A4, delta 0 drifting to +2, 102 rows, 20 exact, 74 register-only
  --- the second nested pointer fixup: one extra field reload.
- +0x9A4..+0xB3C, delta 0, 102 rows, 48 exact, 54 register-only --- temp ring
  permutation only.

Three of these were fixed by source changes; the fixes are independent and
each was measured alone.

**1. The header hunk (worth 311 positional words).** With `object->unk8D`
assigned after `object->unk91`/`unk93`, the scheduler has two independent byte
stores in reach of the earlier floating-point hazard and fills it; the target
emits the hazard `nop` instead and uses the same two stores to cover the *later*
byte-load delay. Moving the `unk8D` assignment ahead of the two zeroing stores
reproduces the target's choice. Measured alone this *grows* the object by one
word (719 -> 720), which is the point: it restores an instruction the candidate
was missing, and reveals that two other instructions were surplus.

**2. The nested pointer fixups (worth 2 instructions and ~180 words).** Both
`unk4C` and `unk48` fixups test an inner field and then recompute from it. The
candidate re-read the field inside the body, giving one load more than the
target, which reuses the value it tested. Holding it in a carrier (the dead
`offset` local; a fresh local was not needed) removes the reload. A
six-carrier x two-operand-order x three-site-set lattice (72 compiled and
scored candidates) ranks `offset` first at 187, `index`/`resultSize`/`size` at
189 and `loadType` at 287; applying it to *both* sites overshoots to a -4 size
delta, so exactly one carrier is right and the second surplus instruction is
still unlocated.

**3. `resultSize = 0;` before `size >>= 2;`** is worth one further word (187 ->
186) and one more aligned-exact row.

### What is now exact, and what is left

- **The stack frame and every home.** Twenty distinct sp-relative homes, 53
  accesses, identical offsets and identical access widths on both sides. This
  was not true of the baseline in any prior pass.
- **Relocations:** 99 on each side, 97 agreeing in offset and type and 97
  agreeing in full identity, against 47 exact identities recorded before.
- The first 110 words and the last two regions are in phase.

The 186 remaining words are, in order of size:

- **The saved-register exchange, 54 rows.** The target holds `size` in `s0` and
  `newObject` in `s1`; the candidate has them the other way round, and a second
  exchange (`s0`/`s3`) runs from +0x7A4. **Declaration order does not decide
  this**: a complete move-one lattice over all fifteen declarations (210
  compiled candidates, every permutation reachable by one move) is flat at 186
  with an unchanged frame. Six placements of the `newObject` definition are
  also flat or much worse (389-451). The axis is therefore neither the
  declaration list nor the definition point of `newObject`; look for the web
  that interferes with `size` across the allocation call.
- **The temp ring, 76 rows**, a position-dependent permutation of `t1`-`t9`
  rather than one global rotation, plus `a2`->`t0` at the reloaded asset
  pointer and `v0`->`t0` in the tail.
- **17 rows that differ in kind**, concentrated at the +0x1B4 header and the
  copy-loop preamble at +0x6DC, where the target keeps the scaled induction
  variable as a shift of a register that is provably zero and the candidate
  folds it to a move.

### Next lever

Locate the second surplus instruction (the one that makes the two-carrier form
overshoot) at the copy-loop preamble around +0x6DC, then re-test the two-site
carrier form. Two handles on it are already measured and closed. The missing
word is the scaled induction variable: the target computes `resultSize * 4` as
a real shift of a register that holds zero, and the candidate folds it away.
Spelling it `resultSize << 2` instead of `resultSize * 4` is **completely
inert** --- byte-identical objects at both sites, across every other variant
tried. Moving the `resultSize = 0;` definition to any of four earlier anchors
does restore the instruction (size delta returns to +4, and to 0 once the
second fixup carrier is added), but it costs far more than it buys: the first
mismatch retreats from +0x1B4 to +0xAC or +0xB8, breaking the exact opening 110
words, and the score lands at 547-624 against 186. The handle must therefore
block the fold without moving the definition. After that the residual is entirely saved-register and temp-ring
naming, and the axis for the saved pair is *not* declaration order --- that is
measured, not assumed.
